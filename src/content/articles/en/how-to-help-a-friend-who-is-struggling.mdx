## What should I say when a friend first tells me?

Telling someone is often the hardest and bravest step. Your first words will shape whether they ever say it again, so start with thanks.

- “Thank you for telling me. That took courage.”
- “You’re not the only one, and I’m not going anywhere.”
- “How can I help? Would it help if I checked in with you?”

Scripture asks us to respond gently, and humbly:

> Brothers, even if a man is caught in some fault, you who are spiritual must restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness, looking to yourself so that you also aren’t tempted. Bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ.
>
> Galatians 6:1–2

## What should I avoid?

- **Shock or disgust.** It tells them they were right to hide.
- **Asking for details** of what they saw. You don’t need them, and they can stir things up for both of you.
- **Lectures.** They almost certainly know it’s wrong already. What they need is hope.
- **Telling others** without their permission. Gossip destroys trust.
- **Trying to be their only answer.** You can walk with them, but you can’t change their heart. That’s God’s work.

Shame is one of the biggest reasons people don’t talk about this. Clinicians note that, because of the shame involved, many people never discuss it openly ([Zhu and colleagues, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1621136/full)). Researchers likewise urge that help be given “in a non-judgmental way that doesn’t induce shame” ([American Psychological Association, 2020](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2020/02/religious-moral-porn-addiction)). Your calm response can be the start of their freedom.

## How can I keep walking with them?

> Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their labor. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow; but woe to him who is alone when he falls, and doesn’t have another to lift him up.
>
> Ecclesiastes 4:9–10

- **Agree on a rhythm.** A short message every day or a call every week. Ask “How are you, really?” and wait for the real answer.
- **Pray together**, not just for them.
- **Talk about the whole of life**, not only this struggle: sleep, stress, loneliness, purpose. Those are often where slips begin.
- **Help with practical steps**, like charging the phone outside the bedroom or setting up filters, if they ask.
- **Be an accountability partner** if you’re both willing: someone who sees honest check-ins and is there in the hard moments.

## What if they slip again?

Expect it, and don’t make it the end of the story. “A friend loves at all times” (Proverbs 17:17). Respond to a slip the way God responds to us: with mercy that is “new every morning” (Lamentations 3:23). Thank them for being honest, ask what led to it, and help them take one small next step. Never hold their honesty against them, or they’ll learn to stop telling you.

## When should I encourage more help?

Gently encourage your friend to see a pastor or counsellor if they’ve tried many times to stop and can’t, if it’s harming their health, work, studies or relationships, or if they feel hopeless. That isn’t a failure on your part. It’s love.

If your friend talks about hurting themselves, take it seriously. Stay with them, and help them contact a [crisis line](/get-help) or local emergency services straight away.

If your friend is under 18, encourage them to talk to a parent, a pastor or a school counsellor too. Holy Auramaxing is only for adults.

## How do I look after myself?

Walking with someone can be heavy. Make sure you have support of your own, keep your own boundaries, and be honest about your own struggles too. “Iron sharpens iron” (Proverbs 27:17): you’ll both grow.
