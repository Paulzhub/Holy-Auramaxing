The Bible treats lust as desire that turns a person into something to use, and Jesus says it begins in the heart, not only in what we do (Matthew 5:27–28). Sexual desire itself is God’s good gift; lust is that gift bent away from love. And Scripture never leaves us there: in Christ there is full forgiveness, and the Spirit gives power to live differently.

## Is sexual desire itself sinful?

No. God made us as embodied, sexual people, and called it good.

> God created man in his own image. In God’s image he created him; male and female he created them.
>
> Genesis 1:27

> God saw everything that he had made, and, behold, it was very good.
>
> Genesis 1:31

Attraction is not lust. Noticing that someone is beautiful is not lust. The Bible’s concern is what we do with desire: whether it serves love and faithfulness, or whether it takes a person made in God’s image and turns them into an object for our own use.

## What did Jesus say about lust?

> You have heard that it was said, ‘You shall not commit adultery;’ but I tell you that everyone who gazes at a woman to lust after her has committed adultery with her already in his heart.
>
> Matthew 5:27–28

Jesus moves the question from the outside to the inside. The issue isn’t only what we do but the deliberate, lingering look that wants what isn’t ours. The next verses use deliberately shocking images (“If your right eye causes you to stumble, pluck it out”) to say: take this seriously and make real changes. Jesus is not telling anyone to harm their body. He’s calling us to cut off whatever feeds the habit.

## Why does lust matter so much?

Because people matter so much. Lust takes someone God loves and reduces them to a body. And because our own bodies matter too:

> Or don’t you know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, for you were bought with a price. Therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God’s.
>
> 1 Corinthians 6:19–20

Paul calls believers to learn to control their own bodies “in sanctification and honor, not in the passion of lust” (1 Thessalonians 4:4–5). That’s a process of growth, not a test you pass or fail in a day.

## Is being tempted the same as sinning?

No. Jesus himself was tempted, and never sinned.

> For we don’t have a high priest who can’t be touched with the feeling of our infirmities, but one who has been in all points tempted like we are, yet without sin. Let’s therefore draw near with boldness to the throne of grace, that we may receive mercy and may find grace for help in time of need.
>
> Hebrews 4:15–16

James describes how temptation grows into sin when we entertain it: desire “when it has conceived, bears sin” (James 1:14–15). The thought that comes uninvited isn’t the sin. The moment to act is when you notice it, by turning towards God and away from the look.

Feeling deeply troubled by lust also doesn’t mean something is wrong with your mind. Mental health researchers point out that distress coming only from moral conviction is not, by itself, a sign of a disorder ([Zhu and colleagues, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1621136/full)). A tender conscience is a gift. Just don’t let it turn into shame that keeps you hiding.

## How does God help us change?

Not by willpower alone. Scripture puts grace first, and grace teaches us a new way to live.

> For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all men, instructing us to the intent that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we would live soberly, righteously, and godly in this present age;
>
> Titus 2:11–12

> But I say, walk by the Spirit, and you won’t fulfill the lust of the flesh.
>
> Galatians 5:16

In practice, that means filling your mind with what’s good (Romans 12:2; Philippians 4:8), guarding the places and times where you’re weakest, and walking with other believers who know your struggle and pray for you.

## What if I’ve already given in?

Then you’re exactly the kind of person the gospel is for.

> If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous to forgive us the sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.
>
> 1 John 1:9

David prayed after his own sexual sin, “Create in me a clean heart, O God. Renew a right spirit within me” (Psalm 51:10). Paul wrote to people with a past like ours: “Some of you were such, but you were washed” (1 Corinthians 6:11). Your story isn’t over. Confess it, receive forgiveness, tell someone you trust, and take the next step.
