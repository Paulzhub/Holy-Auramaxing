## Why does shame make it harder to stop?

Guilt says, “I did something wrong.” Shame says, “I am something wrong.” Guilt can lead you back to God. Shame pushes you into hiding, and hiding is where this habit grows. Many people get stuck in a loop: a slip, then shame, then secrecy, then loneliness, and then the next slip.

The gospel breaks that loop at the start.

> There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus…
>
> Romans 8:1

Your conscience matters. But how you treat yourself after a slip matters too. In two large studies, people who believed porn was morally wrong were more likely to feel addicted to it, even when they used it no more than others did. The lead researcher’s advice to anyone who wants to cut back was to get help that “doesn’t induce shame” ([American Psychological Association, 2020](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2020/02/religious-moral-porn-addiction)). Grace isn’t a loophole. It’s how change starts.

## What does confession actually look like?

Confession has two directions: up to God, and across to another person.

> If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous to forgive us the sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.
>
> 1 John 1:9

> Confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed.
>
> James 5:16

Pick one person who is mature, kind and able to keep a confidence: a friend, a mentor, a pastor. You don’t need to describe what you saw. “I’ve been struggling with porn, and I don’t want to fight it alone any more” is enough. Then ask them to check in on you regularly. Most people find that the first time they say it out loud, the weight gets lighter.

## How do I get through a strong urge?

An urge feels as if it will keep growing until you give in, but it doesn’t. It rises, peaks and falls, like a wave. God promises a way through:

> No temptation has taken you except what is common to man. God is faithful, who will not allow you to be tempted above what you are able, but will with the temptation also make the way of escape, that you may be able to endure it.
>
> 1 Corinthians 10:13

Some ways of escape that help many people:

- **Name it.** Say quietly, “This is an urge. It will pass.”
- **Breathe slowly** for one minute, and pray a short prayer.
- **Move.** Stand up, leave the room, go for a walk, or put the phone in another room.
- **Reach out.** Text someone, even just “Pray for me, I’m struggling.”
- **Wait ten minutes.** The peak of an urge rarely lasts that long.
- **Fill your mind** with something better (Philippians 4:8).

There’s research behind riding out the wave. In one study with smokers, a short “urge surfing” exercise didn’t make cravings weaker, but people who learned it acted on them less over the next week ([Bowen and Marlatt, 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20025372/)). Learning to notice an urge without obeying it is a skill, and skills grow with practice.

## Which everyday changes make a real difference?

- **Know your patterns.** Many slips come when people are bored, lonely, stressed or tired, late at night, or alone with a phone. Notice yours, and plan for them.
- **Change the setting.** Charge your phone outside the bedroom. Set up content filters on a good day, and let someone you trust hold the password. Our [Get help](/get-help) page explains how.
- **Replace, don’t just remove.** Sleep, exercise, real friendships, serving others and time in Scripture fill the space porn used to take.
- **Count honest days, not perfect ones.** Freedom grows one day at a time.

## What if I slip?

You will probably slip at some point. That doesn’t cancel your progress, and it doesn’t change how God sees you.

> It is because of Yahweh’s loving kindnesses that we are not consumed, because his mercies don’t fail. They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness.
>
> Lamentations 3:22–23

Tell God and your trusted person the truth quickly, ask what led to it, and take one small next step. A slip resets a streak, never a person.

## When should I get more help?

Talk to a pastor or a counsellor if you’ve tried many times to stop and can’t, if it’s crowding out your health, work, studies or relationships, or if you keep going even when it brings you no pleasure. The World Health Organization describes a condition called compulsive sexual behaviour disorder in those terms. It also notes that distress which comes only from moral disapproval is not enough, on its own, for that diagnosis ([Zhu and colleagues, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1621136/full)). In other words, struggling doesn’t automatically make you “an addict”, but you don’t need a label to ask for help. Psychotherapy, including cognitive behavioural therapy, is the help most often recommended.

If you ever think about hurting yourself, please contact a [crisis line](/get-help) or your local emergency number now.
